North Coast Community Watershed Project

Where people get water on the north coast

Who. People who live in Clatsop and Tillamook Counties, especially outside town. The Project put this map together so neighbors, volunteers, and the counties can see the public record in one place.

What. Each shape is a creek. Inside it we counted three public lists: houses that sit outside city limits, wells written in Oregon well logs, and legal rights to take water from a stream. Darker color means a higher count for whichever list you pick.

When. The lists were copied from state and county servers on September 4, 2026. They are a snapshot of the record that day, not a live feed and not a history of every year.

Where. Clatsop and Tillamook only. If a creek runs out of those two counties, the house count stops at the county line.

Why. Forestry chemicals and logging can reach drinking water. Before anyone can talk about risk, we have to know who lives on which creek and whether the paper trail is a well, a stream right, or a city or water-district pipe. A house outside city limits may still be on city water. A well with no stream right is often a groundwater well. Some wells were drilled before Oregon asked for a groundwater right. This map shows the mismatch so those questions can be checked creek by creek. It does not say how any one house drinks.
